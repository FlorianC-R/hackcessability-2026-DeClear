# Accessibility & Design Principles

These are permanent, non-negotiable design principles for the DeClear project. Apply them to every feature, screen, and piece of copy. When a design decision conflicts with these principles, these principles win.

## Who we build for

- The primary users are older adults who may struggle with digital communication.
- Never assume an older adult lacks technical ability. Design for a wide range of comfort levels.
- Preserve user independence and dignity. Never use patronizing language, and never talk down to the user.

## Cognitive accessibility first

- Prioritize cognitive accessibility, not just visual accessibility. Reduce the mental effort required to understand and act.
- Use clear language and short sentences. Prefer plain words over jargon.
- Avoid unnecessary icons, animations, menus, and complex navigation. Keep each screen focused on one task.

## Readability & contrast

- Use large, readable typography and high-contrast text.
- Never rely exclusively on colour to communicate meaning. Pair colour with text, icons, or shape so meaning is still clear in grayscale or for colour-blind users.

## Clarity of actions

- Every button must have a clear, understandable label that says what it does. Avoid vague labels like "OK" or "Go".
- Make important dates, deadlines, and actions visually prominent so they are impossible to miss.

## Trust & transparency

- Always provide a way to inspect the original notification. Users must be able to see the source document behind any simplified or summarized content.
